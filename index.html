A developer career, shipped in five versions

Every release adds something new. Here is what changes as you move from the first build to the long term support version of yourself.

v1.0

The first build

3 to 6 lakh

You learn a language, finish real projects and publish something a stranger can install.

v2.0

Owns a feature

5 to 10 lakh

Tickets turn into features. You handle APIs, state and bugs without being walked through them.

v3.0

Designs, not just codes

9 to 18 lakh

Architecture, testing habits and performance start to matter. Juniors begin asking you questions.

v4.0

Owns the release

16 to 30 lakh

Reviews, mentoring and system thinking. Teams trust you on release day.

v5.0

Leads the product

28 to 50 lakh and up

Lead, architect or founder. You connect business goals with technical choices.

What actually moves the number

Degrees matter less than most people think. These three habits show up again and again in higher offers.

Proof beats promises

A published app, a clean GitHub and a short case study tell a recruiter more than a page of skill logos ever will.

Breadth around depth

Get properly good at one platform, then add design sense, APIs and testing. That combination is hard to replace.

Explaining your choices

People who can say why they built something a certain way get trusted with bigger work, and bigger work pays more.
